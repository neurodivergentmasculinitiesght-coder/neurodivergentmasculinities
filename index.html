What this study is about

This is a Masters research study in medical anthropology. It explores what daily life at university is like for neurodivergent, male-identifying students: their relationships, expectations around being male, whether they feel they belong, and how having or not having a formal diagnosis relates to their access to support.

The study does not approach neurodivergence as a problem to be fixed. It approaches it as a real and varied human experience. The goal is to understand that experience from the inside, through the stories of the people living it.

Rather than viewing you as a subject of research, this study treats your experiences and perspectives as knowledge. The aim is to understand what university life looks like from your point of view, including how you experience neurodivergence, masculinity, relationships, belonging and access to support.

The study asks how neurodivergent, male-identifying students understand and make sense of masculinity and identity, how everyday university spaces and practices relate to experiences of belonging, and how having, not having, or making sense of a formal diagnosis may relate to recognition, identity and access to support.

Who can take part

🧠

You identify as neurodivergent in a way that is meaningful to you

Neurodivergent can include ADHD, autism, dyslexia and other ways of thinking or learning differently. You may use the word yourself, or understand your experiences in your own way.

🎓

You are a Rhodes University student

You're currently enrolled at Rhodes University, either as an undergraduate or postgraduate student, and you are between 18 and 35 years old. You can be from any year, faculty or field of study.

👤

You are male-identifying

This study uses a broad understanding of what it means to identify as male. It is not tied to gender assigned at birth. Any student who identifies as male is welcome.

A formal clinical diagnosis is not required. Self-identification is accepted. The study is also interested in how people recognise, understand and name their own experiences.

Not sure what ‘neurodivergent’ means for you?

Neurodivergent can include ADHD, autism, dyslexia and other ways of thinking or learning differently. You do not need to have a formal diagnosis or a specific way of describing your experiences. You may already use the word ‘neurodivergent’, another word, a word from a language you speak, or no particular word at all.

The study is interested in how you understand these experiences for yourself, and how they connect with everyday university life, relationships, identity, masculinity and belonging.

What participation involves

Participation is flexible. There is no single method everyone must follow. You engage in the way that works best for you. Below is what the study uses and what each involves.

💬

Semi-structured interviews

A conversation about your experiences. Between 60 and 90 minutes. You can receive the questions in advance. You choose where we meet. You can pause or stop at any time.

👁️

Participant observation

The researcher is present in campus spaces over the fieldwork period. This is not surveillance. It is observing how the university environment works in everyday moments.

📸

Photovoice (optional)

You take photos of your daily university life using your own phone and discuss them with the researcher. Completely optional and entirely participant-led.

🗣️

Informal conversations

Casual conversations that happen naturally during observation. Not recorded. The researcher writes notes afterwards. Nothing is attributed to you by name.

How it works, step by step

1

You reach out

Send a message to the research email address. Just introduce yourself. No formal application. No paperwork yet.

2

You receive the information sheet

A plain-language document explaining exactly what participation involves, what your rights are, and how your data will be protected. Take as long as you need to read it.

3

You ask any questions you have

There is no pressure to decide immediately. The researcher will answer any questions before you make any decision.

4

You consent on your own terms

Consent can be written, verbal, or in another format that works for you. Interview questions can be sent to you in advance. You choose where and how we meet.

5

You participate in the way that works for you

At least one interview. Other methods are additional and flexible. You can withdraw from any part of the study at any point without giving a reason.

6

You receive the findings

Once the study is complete, you will be offered a plain-language summary of the key findings and kept informed of any publications or presentations.

Your rights as a participant

  • Participation is entirely voluntary. You will never be pressured to join or to stay.
  • You can withdraw at any point without giving a reason. This has no effect on your academic standing, funding, or relationship with any support service at Rhodes.
  • Your real name will never appear in any research output. Pseudonyms are used consistently across all materials.
  • Everything you share is confidential and used for research purposes only. It will not be shared with Rhodes staff or any other institutional representative.
  • If you engage with photovoice, you retain full control over which images are used. Consent for image use is confirmed again at the point of write-up.
  • If anything comes up during the study that is emotionally difficult, you will be provided with information about the Rhodes University Counselling Centre. Support is always available.
  • You can request access to your own data at any point and ask for it to be withdrawn and destroyed.
  • All data is stored in compliance with the Protection of Personal Information Act No. 4 of 2013 and retained for five years after the study concludes.
